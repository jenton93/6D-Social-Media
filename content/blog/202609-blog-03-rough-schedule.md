# Why we ask for a rough schedule before we can price anything

It's an annoying question, and I know it is. You've got a date, a venue and a
vague idea of what the day looks like. We're asking for a running order you
haven't written yet.

Here's why we ask anyway.

## The kit list is rarely what moves the number

People assume the quote is mostly kit, how many screens, how big, how many
fixtures. That part is real, but it's the stable bit. It doesn't change much
once the room is decided.

What moves the number is **crew hours** and **how long the kit sits on site.**
Both come out of the schedule, and neither can be worked out from a floor plan.

## Two examples of the same show costing different amounts

**Your get-in window.** ‹ASSUMED, replace with a real one› If we can be in the
room the afternoon before, the build runs at a sensible pace with a normal
crew. If the venue can't give us the room until 6am on show day, the same
build has to happen in a third of the time, which means more people, not
fewer, all arriving early.

Counterintuitively, **a shorter get-in usually costs more.** The work doesn't
shrink. You just have to throw bodies at it.

**Your finish time.** A show that comes down at 11pm and needs another room
ready by 8am is a different job from one with a day in between. Sometimes it's
two crews rather than one, because the same people can't do both safely.

## What a rough schedule actually needs to be

Not a running order. Nothing that commits you to anything.

- When can we get into the room, and when do we have to be out?
- What time does the first session start, and roughly when does the day end?
- Is there rehearsal, and when?
- Is it one day or several, and if several, what happens overnight?
- Anything in the room before or after us?

Five lines. That's enough to price properly.

## Why it's worth doing before the quote rather than after

Because otherwise the number moves later, and nobody enjoys that conversation.

A quote priced on assumptions is a quote that changes when the assumptions
turn out to be wrong, usually two weeks out, usually upward, usually when
you've already told your client the figure.

We'd rather ask the awkward question early than send a revised quote late.

## And sometimes the schedule is the thing to change

This is the part worth knowing. Occasionally we'll look at a schedule and say
the cheapest change available isn't to the kit list, it's to ask the venue
for the room three hours earlier.

change saved someone real money?*

That conversation only happens if we see the timings early enough to have it.
