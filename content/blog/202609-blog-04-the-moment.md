# Every large show has a moment

A projector dies. A truck is late. The room isn't ready. Someone changes the
running order at four in the afternoon.

Not might. Will. Anyone who has done enough of these knows the plan is a
starting position rather than a promise, and the people who tell you otherwise
are the ones who haven't done enough of them.

## The question isn't whether it happens

It's what the next ninety seconds look like.

And that isn't a function of how big your supplier is. It's whether the person
standing next to you has done this before, knows your show, and can make the
call without ringing anyone for permission.

⛔ *A real example here would carry the whole post. Something that failed and
what happened next. It doesn't need to be dramatic, and it's better if it
isn't.*

## What that needs, in practice

**Someone senior in the room.** Not on email, not on the end of a phone.
Somebody who can decide, and who will be held to the decision.

**Kit that's already doubled.** Redundancy as standard rather than as a line
item you can decline. The question isn't whether a switcher fails, it's
whether the second one is racked and running when it does.

**A crew who've worked together.** Not five qualified people meeting at 6am.
The difference shows up in the ninety seconds and nowhere else.

**A schedule with room in it.** Most of what looks like a crisis on the day is
a get-in that was twenty minutes tight in the first place.

## The bit nobody puts on a quote

⛔ *Worth adding: what we carry that never gets used, and why it's on the
truck anyway.*

None of that is visible on the day it isn't needed, which is most days. It's
also the entire difference between two quotes that look the same.
