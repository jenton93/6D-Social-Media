# What we do

Six kinds of event, six departments.

*All six sections are the same length and weight, by request. Nothing here
signals which we do most of.*

---

## Conferences

We run the technical side of large conferences, or whichever part of it you
need covering. That means production management across the whole show,
including the rooms we aren't supplying, so there's one schedule and one
person who knows where everything is.

Plenary, breakouts, catering and registration running at once. Presenter
slides come in through one portal by a deadline, formatted and ready in the
room. Rehearsals, with someone senior there when the running order changes.
And the get-in planned around the venue's window rather than ours.

⛔ *Largest conference: delegates, rooms, get-in hours, crew.*

---

## Awards

Awards have to look like television and run to a curfew, so we build and crew
them accordingly. Set and staging built for the camera position as much as the
room, lighting designed for camera rather than just the audience, and
show-calling and playback for VTs, walk-ons, stings and trophy moments.
Content is made in house, so the graphics and the screens are one team.

Then the load-out starts in the main room while a few hundred people are still
in the foyer. Which is why the strike gets planned like part of the show: crew
routes, what comes down when, and where the trucks sit.

⛔ *An example, named or anonymised.*

---

## Exhibitions

One stand for a single exhibitor, or forty stands for the organiser, and
everything in between. Exhibitor stands built and crewed, organiser builds
across a whole show, seminar areas with staging, screens, sound and lighting,
and main room keynote, which is a conference inside an exhibition.

Power distribution across a hall, worked inside the venue's build and break
windows, which are usually the hardest constraint on the job.

---

## Product launches

Short, high-stakes, and usually built to someone else's creative concept.
We'll build to your designer's drawings or to ours, and tell you early where
the room won't allow it.

Single-day builds and short lead times, with set, video, lighting and content
delivered together rather than the content turning up afterwards.

⛔ *The thinnest of the six, because it's the event type I know least about.
Two lines on what you actually bring to a launch and it matches the rest.*

---

## Festivals

Two different jobs, and we do both. We supply production crew and management
into large public festivals, slotting into an existing structure rather than
needing to run it.

And we deliver corporate events built in a festival format, end to end.
Recognition and reward events across multiple stages or areas on an outdoor or
semi-outdoor site, with power distribution across the site and kit specified
for weather.

---

## Install

Permanent and semi-permanent systems: video, lighting, sound and power
installed to stay. What's different is where we come from. We install from a
live events base, so rigging positions, control positions and power are
planned for temporary rigs as well as for the installed system.

The failure we're usually brought in to avoid is a spec that's technically
correct and falls over the first time someone runs a real show in the space.

⛔ *One concrete example of what a non-events integrator misses would land
this.*

### Why this version is different

The first draft explained what each event type involves. Our reader runs these

to.

a show like yours?** It's also more useful, because it's the list a producer
