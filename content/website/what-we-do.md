# What we do

Six kinds of event, six departments.

*All six sections are the same length and weight, by request. Nothing here
signals which we do most of.*

---

## Conferences

Multi-day, multi-room, and a running order that moves.

- Production management across the whole show, including rooms we aren't
  supplying
- Set, staging, video, lighting, sound and power, together or separately
- Plenary, breakouts, catering and registration running at once
- Presenter slides in through one upload portal, formatted and ready in the
  room
- Rehearsals, with someone senior there when the order changes
- The get-in planned around the venue's window, not ours

⛔ *Largest conference: delegates, rooms, get-in hours, crew.*

---

## Awards

A show that has to look like television, then strike while the party's on.

- Set and staging built for the camera position as much as the room
- Show-calling and playback: VTs, walk-ons, stings, trophy moments
- Lighting designed for camera, not just for the audience
- Content made in house, so the graphics and the screens are one team
- A load-out that starts in the main room while a few hundred people are still
  in the foyer
- The strike planned like part of the show: crew routes, what comes down when,
  where the trucks sit

⛔ *An example, named or anonymised.*

---

## Exhibitions

One stand for an exhibitor, or forty stands for the organiser.

- Exhibitor stands, built and crewed. One is a job worth doing
- Organiser builds across a whole show, at forty stands and up
- Seminar areas: staging, screens, sound and lighting
- Main room keynote, which is a conference inside an exhibition
- Power distribution across a hall
- Working inside the venue's build and break windows, usually the hardest
  constraint on the job

---

## Product launches

Short, high-stakes, and usually built to someone else's creative concept.

- Built to your designer's drawings, or ours
- An early answer on what the room will and won't allow
- Single-day builds and short lead times
- Set, video, lighting and content delivered together
- Content produced alongside the technical build rather than after it

⛔ *This section is thinner than the others because I know least about it. Two
lines on what you actually bring to a launch and it matches the rest.*

---

## Festivals

Two different jobs, and we do both.

- **Crew and production management into large public festivals.** We supply
  the people rather than the whole show, slotting into an existing structure
  without needing to run it
- **Corporate events in a festival format, delivered end to end.** Recognition
  and reward events built as a festival rather than a dinner
- Multiple stages or areas across an outdoor or semi-outdoor site
- Power distribution and supply across the site
- Kit specified for weather and for a site with no walls

---

## Install

Permanent and semi-permanent systems, specified by people who run live events.

- Video, lighting, sound and power installed to stay
- Designed from a live events base, so the room works when an event runs in it
- Rigging positions, control positions and power planned for temporary rigs as
  well as the installed system
- The failure we're brought in to avoid: a spec that's technically correct and
  falls over the first time someone runs a real show in the space

this.*

### Why this version is different

The first draft explained what each event type involves. Our reader runs these
for a living, so that reads as either padding or, worse, as being talked down
to.

Everything here now answers one question instead: **what do we actually do on
a show like yours?** It's also more useful, because it's the list a producer
scans to work out whether we cover the gap they've got.
