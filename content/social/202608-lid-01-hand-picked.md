Most crew booking is done by software now. You put in the dates, the roles and
the rate, and it tells you who's available.

I understand why it exists. If you're staffing forty shows a week you can't do
it any other way.

But it's worth being clear about what it's optimising for, because it's three
things: who's free, who's certified, and what they cost. All measurable, all
sensible.

Here's what it can't see.

Whether someone's any good in front of a client. Whether there's a
problem-solver in the team or five people who'll all wait to be told. Whether
anyone's worked in that building before and knows the load-in is a nightmare.
Whether these particular people work well together at 2am when the schedule
has gone.

I've been on shows where every single person was qualified for their role and
it was still a mess, because nobody had thought about the team as a team.

We hand pick crew for the event. It's slower — on a big one it costs me a
couple of hours I'd rather spend somewhere else. And we don't put people we
don't know into critical roles, which occasionally means turning down a job we
can't crew properly.

A form can tell you someone can operate a desk. It can't tell you whether
they'll spot the problem before it becomes one.

**Notes on why it's built this way**

- **Opens on a fact, not a hook.** "Most crew booking is done by software now"
  is a complete thought and it earns the second line without a gimmick
- **Concedes first.** "I understand why it exists" stops it reading as a
  whinge, and makes the criticism land harder because it's clearly not reflex
- **Attacks the system, never the bookers.** Per the rule in
  `09-director-linkedin.md`. No company is named
- **The cost is admitted** — slower, a couple of hours, occasionally turning
  work down. That's what makes it believable. A claim with no downside reads
  as marketing
- **Ends one line early.** The desk line is the last thing; there's no summary
  after it, per `01-voice-and-tone.md` §6
- **Nothing from the banned list.** No "in today's", no "here's the thing", no
  rule-of-three with three equal-length items, no emoji

⛔ **Two things to check before posting**
1. Is "a couple of hours" honest? Swap in the real figure — the specificity is
   the point
2. Have you actually turned down a job you couldn't crew properly? If not, cut
   that clause. It's the strongest line in the post and the most damaging one
   to be wrong about
