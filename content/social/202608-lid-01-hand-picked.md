Three people, all booked for the same show, all fine on paper.

The first one ticks every box. Knows every bit of kit, done events this size,
worked in that building before. On paper he's the obvious call.

He also can't talk to a client, and when the plan moves he can't move with it.

The second one doesn't really know the kit. Give him twenty minutes and he'll
work it out. What he'll actually do is spot the problem before anyone else
has, ring someone he knows who can fix it, and then explain to the client
what's happening in a way that leaves them calmer than before they asked.

The third one is quiet. Sits at the back. Everything on the list gets done and
nobody has to ask him for any of it.

A booking system sees the first one. He's the only one it can measure.

You need all three in the room, and that's the bit that isn't on any form. How
do you record that two people work well together? Or that someone's good with
a client who's nervous?

We hand pick crew for the event. It's slower, and on a big one it costs me a
couple of hours I'd rather spend elsewhere. But I've never had to explain to a
client why the person running their show had never seen a room like theirs.

A form can tell you someone can operate a desk. It can't tell you whether
they'll spot the problem before it becomes one.

**Notes on why it's built this way**

- **Opens on the three people, not on the argument.** "Three people, all
  booked for the same show, all fine on paper" is a complete thought that
  makes you want line three. No question-hook, no "here's the thing"
- **The archetypes are the post.** They're yours, they're original, and
  nobody else in this market is framing crew this way. That's what makes it
  worth reading rather than agreeing with
- **Never mentions AI or software companies by name.** The system is the
  subject, not any firm
- **Attacks the system, never the bookers.** Per the rule in
  `09-director-linkedin.md`. No company is named
- **The cost is admitted**, slower, a couple of hours, occasionally turning
  work down. That's what makes it believable. A claim with no downside reads
  as marketing
- **Ends one line early.** The desk line is the last thing; there's no summary
  after it, per `01-voice-and-tone.md` §6
- **Nothing from the banned list.** No "in today's", no "here's the thing", no
  rule-of-three with three equal-length items, no emoji

⛔ **Two things to check before posting**
1. Is "a couple of hours" honest? Swap in the real figure, the specificity is
   the point
2. The three archetypes are described as "he". If the real people you had in
   mind aren't all men, vary it, and it reads better if they're not identical
   anyway
