# Director Posts on LinkedIn

The company page is necessary. The directors' profiles are where the reach
actually is.

A company page post typically reaches a low single-digit percentage of
followers. A personal post from someone with an engaged network routinely
reaches several times that, because LinkedIn's feed is built to show people
people. If we only do one of these well, it should be this one.

---

## Roles

| Who | Posts | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Director A | 2 per week | Primary voice. Point of view + what they're seeing in the market |
| Director B | 1 per week | Second voice. Different angle — operations, delivery, client-side |
| Company page | 3 per week | The record. Blog, proof, announcements |

Two directors posting once or twice a week beats one director posting daily,
because two voices reach two different networks and neither burns out.

If there's only one director willing to post, that's fine — one is enough.
One is enormously better than none. Don't wait for both.

---

## The rule that makes this work

**Directors do not post company marketing.** The moment a personal profile
starts reading like a press release, its reach collapses and it doesn't come
back easily.

| Company page posts | Director posts |
|---|---|
| "We've published a guide to X" | "I got asked this three times last month, so I wrote it down" |
| "Our team completed a project for…" | "We nearly got this one wrong. Here's the bit I'd do differently" |
| "6D Group is pleased to announce…" | "We've hired someone, and I want to explain why this role and not another" |
| Third person, considered | First person, specific, occasionally uncertain |

Same underlying material. Completely different register. The director version
is what the director would actually say out loud.

---

## What a director should post about

In rough order of what works:

1. **Something that happened this week.** A conversation, a problem, a
   decision, a number that surprised them. Specificity is the entire trick.
2. **A view about how the industry works.** Especially one that runs against
   the received wisdom. Must be arguable.
3. **A mistake, and what changed as a result.** Consistently the highest
   engagement post any founder writes, and the one everyone avoids.
4. **Something they learned from a client.** Credits the client, shows we
   listen, and it's genuinely useful.
5. **A short answer to a question they get asked constantly.** Same material
   as Pillar 1, in first person.
6. **Occasionally — the company's work.** No more than one post in five, and
   framed as what they learned doing it rather than what we sell.

**What not to post:** motivational content, reposted company announcements,
"delighted to share", industry-award selfies without a point, anything with
a graphic that says "TOP 5 TIPS".

## Getting the words out of a busy director

Nobody senior has time to write two posts a week, and the ones written to
order always read like it. Three options, in order of how well they work:

**1. The voice-note method** *(recommended)*
Director records 60–90 seconds on their phone about something from that week.
No structure, no script — just talking. That gets transcribed and edited into
a post that keeps their actual phrasing, their actual opinions, and the bits
where they go off on a tangent. **Their words, tidied — never rewritten into
marketing prose.** Director approves or amends before it posts.

Cost to the director: about four minutes a week.

**2. The 20-minute monthly interview**
One call, recorded. Ask what's annoyed them, what's surprised them, what
they've had to explain more than once. That's 6–8 posts of raw material in
one sitting.

**3. Written by them, edited by us**
Best output if they'll do it. Most likely to slip.

Whichever route: **the director always reads it before it goes out**, and
anything they wouldn't say out loud gets cut. One post that sounds like a
marketing department undoes months of credibility.

## Mechanics

- **Optimise the profiles first.** A director's headline, About and banner do
  more work than the next ten posts — see `08-profile-buildout.md` for the
  same treatment applied to personal profiles: headline says what they do for
  whom (not "Director at 6D Group Ltd"), About written in first person,
  featured section pointing at the site.
- Directors and team engage with the company page post within the first hour,
  with a real comment.
- The company page should **not** blanket-comment on director posts — it reads
  as coordinated and it flattens the reach.
- Directors reply to every comment on their own posts, in their own voice.
  This is non-delegable; it's the part people can tell.
- Track director posts in the same calendar and score them the same way. They
  get their own rows in `metrics-log.csv` with `channel=li-personal`.
- Expect director posts to out-score company posts. When they do, the
  allocation shifts toward them — same rule as everything else here.

## Personal profile checklist (per director)

- [ ] Headline: what they do and who for, not their job title
- [ ] Banner: consistent with company branding
- [ ] About: first person, 3–4 short paragraphs, ends with how to reach them
- [ ] Current role lists **6D Group Ltd** (links profile to the company page)
- [ ] Featured section: the site, best blog post, best proof piece
- [ ] Contact info visible
- [ ] Creator mode on
- [ ] Custom URL claimed
