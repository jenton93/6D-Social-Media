# Voice & Tone — Writing Copy That Reads As Human

This is the most important file in the repo. Every post, page and article gets
checked against it before it ships. If copy fails this, it doesn't go out —
regardless of how good the strategy behind it was.

---

## 1. The standard

A reader should never be able to tell whether a person or a machine wrote it.
The test isn't "is this well written" — a lot of AI copy is technically well
written. The test is **"would a specific person, with a specific opinion, on a
specific Tuesday, have written this sentence?"**

Human writing has:
- A point of view that could be disagreed with
- Detail that's too specific to be generic
- Uneven rhythm — some short sentences. Some that run on a bit longer because
  the thought needed the extra room
- The occasional imperfection left in on purpose

AI writing has: balance, symmetry, hedging, and an allergy to committing.

---

## 2. Banned constructions

These are the tells. Don't use them, don't paraphrase around them.

**Banned openers**
- "In today's fast-paced world…" / "In an increasingly digital landscape…"
- "As businesses navigate…" / "In the ever-evolving world of…"
- "Let's dive in" / "Let's unpack" / "Buckle up"
- Any opener that describes the era rather than the subject

**Banned phrases**
- "It's not just X, it's Y" — and every variant of it
- "delve", "leverage" (as a verb), "utilise", "robust", "seamless",
  "cutting-edge", "game-changer", "unlock", "supercharge", "elevate",
  "empower", "revolutionise", "transform your…", "at the end of the day"
- "The truth is…" / "Here's the thing:" / "And that's the point."
- "In conclusion" / "To sum up" / "Ultimately"
- "That's where we come in." — instant credibility loss
- "curated", "bespoke", "holistic", "synergy", "best-in-class"

**⚠️ Register: quiet, not loud**

This is a white-label business working behind agencies and in-house teams.
Producers who buy that way are the people most put off by a supplier showing
off, and they will read confidence in restraint rather than in volume.

So: state facts, don't make claims. Let the reader draw the conclusion.

| Too loud | Right |
|---|---|
| "Big shows. The same people, every time." | "Event production." |
| "That one doesn't bend." | Just say the rule and stop |
| "We make the content, not just play it" | "We make the content as well as run the screens" |
| "That's the difference between forty people on site and a team of forty" | *(cut, it's a flourish)* |

**The exception is the directors' own phrasing.** "Crew are hand picked for the
event" and "calculated audio, not fly and hope" stay exactly as they are.
Those aren't boasts, they're how these two talk, and the character is the
point. The rule is about writer's flourishes, not about voice.

**Banned punctuation**
- **Em dashes. None, anywhere.** Comma, full stop or colon instead. If the
  sentence needs an em dash to work, the sentence needs rewriting
- Never publish turnover or growth percentages. Scale is years, per-show crew,
  departments and named work

**Banned structures**
- The rule-of-three list where every item is the same length
  (~~"faster, smarter, stronger"~~)
- Rhetorical question → one-line answer → rhetorical question → one-line answer
- Ending every paragraph on a punchy fragment. For emphasis.
- Perfectly parallel bullet points that all start with a verb and all run to
  the same length
- Bolding a phrase in the middle of a sentence for emphasis, more than once
  per page

**Banned emoji patterns**
- 🚀 ✨ 💡 🔥 as bullet markers
- Emoji at the start of every line of a LinkedIn post
- The "🧵" thread emoji unless it's genuinely a thread

## 3. What to do instead

**Open on the specific.** Start mid-thought, with a fact, a number, a moment,
or something someone actually said.

> ❌ "In today's competitive market, businesses need to stand out."
> ✅ "A client asked me last week why their tender scored 68 when the one
>    before it scored 91. Same team. Same template."

**Commit to a position.** If a sentence would be true for any company in any
industry, cut it. Say the thing that only we would say.

**Use real numbers and real nouns.** "Three weeks", "£40k", "a 14-person
team", "the Tuesday stand-up" — not "significant time savings" or "a range of
stakeholders".

**Vary sentence length deliberately.** Read it aloud. If it has a steady beat
all the way through, break it.

**Leave the seams in.** Contractions. Starting a sentence with "And" or "But".
An aside in brackets. A sentence that trails off where the thought did.

**Write like you're explaining it to one person you respect,** not addressing
an audience. Second person singular, not "businesses" or "organisations".

**Cut the last line.** Most drafts end with a summarising flourish that adds
nothing. Delete it — the piece almost always ends stronger one line earlier.

## 4. House style

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Spelling | UK English (organise, colour, programme) |
| Person | "We" for the company, "you" for the reader. Never "6D Group believes…" |
| Contractions | Yes — we're, don't, it's, you'll |
| Sentence case | Headings and subject lines in sentence case, not Title Case |
| Numbers | Spell out one to nine, numerals from 10 up. Always numerals for money, %, dates |
| Dates | 4 March 2026 (no ordinal suffix) |
| Oxford comma | No, unless it removes genuine ambiguity |
| Exclamation marks | One per month across all channels, and it had better earn it |
| Jargon | Allowed if our buyer uses it in their own meetings. Banned if it only exists in marketing decks |

## 5. Tone by channel

Same voice everywhere. The dial that moves is formality and length.

| Channel | Dial | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Considered, direct, first-person | A practitioner sharing what they've noticed. No hooks-and-hashtag-spam. |
| Blog | Explanatory, generous, evidenced | Teaching, with the working shown |
| Website | Plain, confident, concrete | Short sentences. Says what we do without adjectives doing the lifting |
| Email | Conversational, one idea | Reads like it was typed, not designed |
| Instagram / video | Warmer, more human, faces and places | Behind-the-work, not stock photography |

## 6. The pre-publish check

Every piece gets read against these six before it goes out. Anything that
fails goes back.

1. **Specificity** — is there at least one detail here that could only come
   from us?
2. **Position** — does this say something a competitor wouldn't also say?
3. **Rhythm** — read aloud, does it have an uneven, human cadence?
4. **Banned list** — clean against section 2?
5. **First line** — does it earn the second line without a gimmick?
6. **Last line** — would the piece be better if we deleted it?

Log the result in the piece's front matter (`voice_check: pass`).
