# Channel Strategy

## Accounts

| Channel | Handle / page | Role |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn (company) | **6D Group Ltd** | Primary. Where buyers are and where enquiries come from. |
| LinkedIn (personal) | **Directors** | Highest-reach channel we have. Own doc: `09-director-linkedin.md`. |
| Instagram | **@6dgroupuk** | Secondary. Proof, personality, work-in-progress, people. |
| Facebook | **6D Group Ltd** | Local search, reviews, and the audience that isn't on LinkedIn. |
| Website blog | 6d-group.com/blog | The asset we own. Everything else points here. |

> Everything above is organic-first. No paid until we have three months of
> scored organic data telling us which pieces are worth putting money behind.

**⚠️ This is a trade audience — 90% of business comes from in-house AV teams
and agencies.** That makes LinkedIn, company page and directors together,
effectively the only channel that can generate enquiries.

Instagram and Facebook still earn their place, but their job is **credibility,
reassurance and crew recruitment** — not leads. A producer who's about to
trust us with a 2,000-delegate show will look us up, and what they find has to
look like a working production company. Score both on Tier 2 and Tier 3 only
(`06-analytics-framework.md`) and don't panic when they produce no enquiries.
That isn't failure; it's the job they were given.

The upside of a trade audience: the market is small and knowable. There are
only so many event agencies and in-house AV teams in the UK. **A hundred
followers who are the right hundred is a complete result** — judge reach by
who, never by how many.

## Cadence

Agreed operating rhythm — **1 blog post a week, 1–2 social posts a week**,
with the blog promoted on social.

That resolves to **three social touchpoints per week**:

| Slot | Day | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Blog promotion** | Thursday | LinkedIn + Instagram | The week's blog post, rewritten natively per channel (never a bare link with "New blog post 👇") |
| **B — Original post** | Tuesday | LinkedIn | Standalone idea. Not a teaser for anything. |
| **C — Original post** *(optional)* | Friday or Monday | Instagram | Visual / people / behind-the-work |

Facebook receives the Slot A blog post and the Slot C visual post, with the
caption re-cut for that audience (see below). It doesn't generate its own
slot — but it isn't left empty either.

**Director posts sit on top of this**, not inside it — 2 a week from Director
A, 1 from Director B, written in first person and never as company marketing.
They're the biggest reach lever in the plan; see `09-director-linkedin.md`.

Slot C is the flex slot. Two-post weeks are fine; three is the target once the
system is running. Consistency beats volume — a missed week costs more reach
than a mediocre post does.

**Non-negotiables**
- Never publish the same copy on both channels. Same idea, rewritten.
- The blog-promotion post must stand alone as a useful post even if nobody
  clicks. If it only makes sense as a link, it's a bad post.
- One link per LinkedIn post, in the post body (LinkedIn's link penalty is
  overstated; the "link in comments" trick now reads as a tell).

## LinkedIn

**What it's for:** being the company our buyer already recognises before they
have a need.

**Formats, in order of what usually works for a small B2B account**
1. **Text post, 120–180 words** — one idea, opened with a specific moment or
   number. The workhorse. Most weeks this is Slot B.
2. **Text + single image** — a photo of real work, a whiteboard, a screenshot.
   Not stock. Not a quote card.
3. **Document / carousel (5–8 slides)** — for anything that's a process, a
   checklist or a before/after. High save rate, and saves correlate better
   with enquiries than likes do.
4. **Native video, 30–90s** — vertical, subtitled, face to camera. Highest
   effort, highest variance. Test in the 10% Bets allocation.

**Mechanics**
- First two lines are all anyone sees before "…see more". They must carry a
  complete, specific thought — not a cliffhanger.
- Line breaks every 1–2 sentences. Walls of text die.
- 3–5 hashtags maximum, at the end, all genuinely relevant. Never inline.
- Reply to every comment within 2 hours, with a sentence that adds something.
  Comment replies are the single cheapest reach lever available.
- Team should comment meaningfully on the company post within the first hour —
  meaningfully, not "Great post!". Empty pods are visible and they cost trust.
- Post 08:00–09:30 UK on Tue/Wed/Thu as the starting hypothesis. Prove or
  disprove it with 6 weeks of data, then stop debating it.

## Instagram (@6dgroupuk)

**What it's for:** proving there are real people doing real work here. It is a
credibility channel, not a lead channel — score it on Tier 2/3, not Tier 1.

**Formats**
- **Reels (15–40s)** — the only format with meaningful organic reach. Process,
  time-lapse, a person explaining one thing.
- **Carousels** — a job or project walked through, 6–10 frames.
- **Stories** — daily-ish, low effort, unpolished on purpose. Polls and
  question stickers here are the fastest audience-research tool we have; the
  answers feed the monthly diagnosis.
- **Grid single posts** — the finished thing, shot properly.

**Mechanics**
- Caption first line is the hook — it's what shows in feed.
- Write captions long enough to say something. Short captions with a wall of
  hashtags read as an account that has nothing to say.
- 8–12 hashtags, mixed reach tiers, in the caption. Refresh the set monthly.
- Save + share are the metrics that matter here. Likes are noise.
- Highlights: **Work**, **Team**, **How we do it**, **Questions**.

## Facebook

**What it's for:** three jobs, none of them "another feed to fill".

1. **Search and trust.** People who hear our name look us up, and a stale page
   with a 2019 cover photo is worse than no page. It has to look current.
2. **Reviews.** Facebook recommendations still carry weight and still surface
   in search. Ask every satisfied client for one — this is the highest-value
   thing the page does.
3. **Reach outside LinkedIn.** Local network, past clients, word-of-mouth
   referrers, and anyone under- or over-indexed on LinkedIn.

**What gets posted**
- Slot A (the blog post) — same idea, warmer and plainer than the LinkedIn
  version. Facebook tolerates a longer, more conversational caption.
- Slot C (the visual post) — cross-posted from Instagram, but with the caption
  rewritten. Auto-cross-posting with Instagram hashtags still attached is the
  clearest possible signal that nobody is minding the page.
- Occasional page-only posts: milestones, hires, a thank-you to a client,
  anything local.

**Mechanics**
- Link posts genuinely are throttled here, so lead with the thought and put
  the link at the end. The link preview does the visual work — make sure blog
  posts have a proper Open Graph image and title (see `04-website-plan.md`).
- 1–2 hashtags at most, or none. Facebook isn't a hashtag platform and heavy
  tagging reads as spam.
- Turn on and monitor Messenger. An unanswered message sitting for four days
  shows publicly as a slow response rate.
- Score it on Tier 2 and Tier 3 only, same as Instagram, plus review count as
  its own tracked metric.

## What we're deliberately not doing

- **X/Twitter** — our buyer isn't making decisions there and it's a per-week
  cost we can't justify at this volume.
- **TikTok** — revisit only if Instagram Reels clear a 55 score consistently,
  which would prove short-form is working before we open a second front.
- **Newsletter** — worth doing, but not until there are ~12 blog posts to draw
  on. Capture emails from day 1 regardless; publish from month 4.
