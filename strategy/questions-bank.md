# Questions Bank

The questions clients actually ask. This list is a year of blog posts, most of
the website FAQ, and the objection-handling on every service page.

> **These twenty are my guesses**, drafted from everything in the brief. They
> are almost certainly not how anyone actually phrases them.
>
> **The value is in the real wording.** "Why do you need the room from 6am?"
> is a post. "What are the benefits of adequate get-in time?" is not. Rewrite
> them in the words you actually hear, delete the ones nobody asks, and add
> the ones I've missed — those will be the best ones on the list.
>
> Never close this file. Every time someone asks something, it goes in.

## The ones I can't guess, and they're the best ones

Three prompts that will produce better questions than anything above:

1. **What did someone ask you last week** that you've now answered more than
   twice?
2. **What does a client push back on** in nearly every quote?
3. **What do people get wrong** so consistently that you've stopped being
   surprised by it?

⛔ *Even five real ones, in the asker's words, are worth more than my twenty.*

---

## How this becomes content

- **One question = one blog post.** Answered properly, no hedging
- **The answer, cut to 120 words = a social post.** The question alone is
  usually the hook
- **Three or four per service page** as "Questions we get asked"
- **Sales use:** when a prospect asks it again, send the post. That's the
  clearest sign a question was worth answering

Priority order: whichever questions come up *before* someone commits. Those
are the ones costing us work when they go unanswered.
