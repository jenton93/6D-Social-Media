# Blog Plan

One post a week, every week. The blog is the only content asset we own
outright — social platforms rent us an audience, the blog compounds.

---

## Why weekly is the right number

52 posts a year is enough to cover every question a buyer has, several times
over, and enough for search to treat the site as active. It's also just about
sustainable for a small team, which matters more than ambition: a blog that
runs weekly for a year beats one that runs three times a week for six weeks
and then stops. The stop is what does the damage.

**Protect the cadence over the length.** A good 700-word post published on
Thursday is worth more than a great 2,000-word post that never gets finished.

### ⚠️ Read this before committing to weekly

This is a 2.5-person business delivering £1m of live events. In September and
October the people who hold the knowledge are on site at 6am, and no blog post
is getting written that week. Weekly only works if it's built around that:

- **Bank 8–10 posts across July, August and the Christmas gap.** Content
  production is a quiet-season activity here. Treat those weeks as the factory
- **Peak-season weeks publish from the bank, not from a draft**
- **Voice notes over writing.** Ninety seconds in the van, transcribed and
  edited, keeps the specificity that makes this content worth reading. Waiting
  for someone to sit down and write costs the whole cadence
- If weekly still can't hold, drop to **fortnightly and hold it for years**.
  In a market where we're selling consistency, publishing weekly for two
  months and then going quiet argues against us. Slower and unbroken wins

## Post types

| Type | Length | Purpose | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The answer** | 800–1,500 | One question from the questions bank, answered fully | 2 per month |
| **The case study** | 600–1,000 | One job, start to finish, with numbers | 1 per month |
| **The opinion** | 600–1,200 | A position on how our industry works | 1 per month |
| **The guide** | 2,000+ | Definitive on one topic. Built to rank and to be sent by sales | 1 per quarter, replacing an "answer" slot |

## Choosing topics

In priority order:

1. **The questions bank** (`strategy/questions-bank.md`) — real questions from
   real clients. Always start here. These convert because someone has already
   proved they're worth answering.
2. **Search Console data** — from month 3 onward, the queries we're already
   getting impressions for but ranking 8–20 on. That's the cheapest traffic
   available: we're nearly there already.
3. **Sales-call objections** — anything a prospect pushes back on twice
   becomes a post, and the salesperson sends it next time.
4. **What the audience asked for** — comments, DMs, Instagram question
   stickers. Direct quotes make the best headlines because they're already
   in the reader's words.
5. **Calendar-driven** — regulation changes, deadlines, seasonal cycles.
   Publish 6–8 weeks ahead of the date, not on it.

**Not** "trending topics in [industry] 2026" or anything generated by asking
what a competitor blogged about. That's how you end up with 52 posts nobody
asked for.

## Structure

```
H1        — the question or claim, in plain words
Intro     — 2–3 sentences. The specific situation this applies to.
            No throat-clearing, no "in today's landscape"
Body      — H2 per sub-point. Short paragraphs. Concrete examples
Proof     — a number, a job, a screenshot, a quote
The honest bit — when this doesn't apply, or what the trade-off is.
            This single section does more for credibility than the
            rest of the post combined
Next step — one, specific. Not "get in touch to learn more"
```

**Every post must contain at least one thing a competitor couldn't have
written** — a number from our work, a client's actual words, something we got
wrong. That's the difference between a blog and a content farm.

## Publishing workflow

| Day | Step |
|---|---|
| Thu | Topic confirmed from the month's brief; outline written |
| Fri | Draft — fast and rough. Don't edit while drafting |
| Mon | Edit against `01-voice-and-tone.md`. Read aloud. Cut 20% |
| Tue | Internal review — factual accuracy, client permissions, the honest bit |
| Wed | Format, images, alt text, meta, internal links, OG image, UTMs |
| **Thu** | **Publish**, then the social cut-downs go out (Slot A) |

Work one week ahead. Two is better. The week you're writing the post you're
publishing that day is the week the quality drops.

---

## Every post ships with its social cut-downs

Written at the same time as the post, not later, and never a bare link:

- **LinkedIn (company)** — 120–180 words that stand alone as a useful post.
  Someone who doesn't click should still have got something.
- **LinkedIn (director)** — same idea, first person, one line about why they
  bothered writing it.
- **Instagram** — a carousel of the key points, or a Reel of one point.
  Caption, not a screenshot of the article.
- **Facebook** — warmer, longer, plainer. Link at the end.

That's one blog post doing four jobs, and it's how a weekly blog produces a
full social calendar without inventing anything.

## Measuring

Blog posts are scored on a 90-day lag, not 30 — organic search needs the time.
Tracked per post: sessions, average position for target keyword, scroll depth,
clicks to a service or contact page, enquiries attributed.

Quarterly: update the top 5 posts rather than writing new ones. Refreshing a
post that already ranks reliably out-performs a new post that doesn't.
