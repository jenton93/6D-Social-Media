# The Three Jobs

Website and social aren't doing one thing. They're doing three, for three
different audiences, and each needs different content and a different measure
of success.

**Every page and every post gets tagged with one of these** (`job:` in front
matter). If a piece doesn't have a job, it doesn't get made.

The reason this matters more than it sounds: **the three jobs succeed on
completely different timescales, and judging one by another's yardstick is how
content plans get abandoned six months in.**

---

## Job 1 — NUDGE · existing clients

> Show the people who already buy from us the departments they aren't buying.

**Who:** QEII Live, Dancing Squirrel, Evolution International, Select Brief,
and everyone else on the books. Most take set & staging or video. Few take all
six.

**Why it matters:** pick-and-mix means the second sale is far cheaper than the
first — the relationship, the trust and the show knowledge already exist. This
is the cheapest route to the £500k growth target and it needs the least
content.

**What it looks like**
- One piece a month on a department they're probably not buying, written for
  someone who already knows how we work
- Lighting content especially — we came up as a lighting company and clients
  who buy staging often have no idea
- "Here's what changes when it's on the same contract as the rest" — the
  practical argument for consolidating, not a sales pitch
- Kit and crew posts. What's in the warehouse, what we've invested in
- Not persuasion. These people are already convinced about us; they just don't
  know the scope

**Where:** LinkedIn (they already follow us), direct email, and the service
pages they land on. Instagram plays a supporting role — clients follow us
there and absorb capability passively.

**How it's measured:** second-department sales. **Not reach.** The audience is
a few dozen people, so impressions will look terrible and the revenue per
impression will be the best of the three jobs. Tag it `second_sale: true` and
score it separately or it will look like the worst-performing content we make.

## Job 2 — COLD · new business

> Become known to agencies and in-house AV teams nobody has recommended us to.

**Who:** the rest of the market. A finite, knowable set — there are only so
many event agencies and in-house AV teams in the UK.

**Why it matters:** it's the only route that grows the addressable base rather
than working the existing one harder. It's also the slowest.

**What it looks like**
- Point of view. What's wrong with how large events get resourced, why scale
  isn't kit, the consistency problem with the big operators
- Expertise content that demonstrates production judgement — the Pillar 1
  material
- Director posts, which out-reach the company page substantially
- SEO on the service pages, for the searches producers actually run

**Where:** LinkedIn, overwhelmingly — company page and directors. Organic
search on the site, second.

**How it's measured:** **enquiries from people nobody recommended us to.**
That needs its own CRM field, set up before launch. Also branded search volume
and follower quality — a hundred followers who are the right hundred is a
complete result.

**⚠️ Expect this to look like failure for six months.** It's the slowest of
the three and the most likely to be abandoned early. Judge it on whether the
right people are showing up, not on how many.

## Job 3 — VERIFY · proving we're real ← *the urgent one*

> Let someone who's already interested confirm we can deliver, and get us
> through their finance team.

**Who:** three arrivals, all mid-decision:

1. Someone we met at a show or an industry conversation, looking us up
   afterwards
2. Someone a producer recommended, checking before they call
3. **An event manager's finance or procurement team**, deciding whether we can
   be set up as a supplier at all

**Why it matters:** this is where work already won on merit is currently being
lost. Clients who experience the service at the QEII Centre ask us to support
them at other venues, and their event manager can't get it agreed, because
someone searches the name and finds nothing. **The problem is absence, not
assessment** — a company with no footprint reads as not a company. Ten years
of recommendations have had nowhere to point.

**It's the fastest win in the plan.** Nothing needs to be persuaded — it just
needs to exist.

**Two readers, and the second is easily forgotten**

| Reader | Wants | Gets |
|---|---|---|
| **The professional** — producer, event manager | Can they handle a show like mine, **and is there anyone behind them if it goes wrong?** | Case studies with real numbers, crew counts, get-in complexity, **the named crew bench**, redundancy and spares, kit |
| **The finance/procurement person** | Are they a real, insurable company? | Company number, VAT, PL and EL insurance, accreditations, RAMS, policies, T&Cs |

The second reader never contacts us. They just say no, and we never find out.
That's why `/credentials` is a main-navigation page and a single downloadable
supplier pack — see `04-website-plan.md`.

**⚠️ Presence first, paperwork second.** The finance-side problem is that
nothing comes up in a search; a website and filled-in profiles settle that,
and the credentials pack is cheap follow-up rather than the fix. The event manager's question
is *"if this goes wrong, is anyone behind them?"* — and no certificate answers
that. They're using the size of Aztec or Encore as a proxy for resilience.
Countering it needs the crew bench, the redundancy and the continuity record
made visible (`00-brand-brief.md` §5a, `03-content-pillars.md` Pillar 4).

**What it looks like**
- The credentials page and supplier information pack
- **The crew bench, named** — the depth behind the core team, and how long
  we've worked together. Currently missing, and the highest-value gap
- Anonymised case studies that prove scale: delegates, halls, get-in hours,
  crew on site, screen area
- A named senior team with real track records
- Consistency across the website, LinkedIn, Facebook, Google Business Profile
  and Companies House. Mismatched addresses and phone numbers are exactly what
  a cautious finance team treats as a red flag
- Reviews and testimonials — a competitive factor in this niche and something
  ten years of clients have never been asked for

**How it's measured:** almost invisibly, and that's the trap. Nobody submits a
form saying "I checked your insurance and proceeded". Proxies:
- `/credentials` page views and pack downloads — the cleanest signal we'll get
- Referral conversion: of the enquiries that arrive, how many convert now
  versus before the site existed
- Simply asking stuck event managers whether it unblocked them. Direct,
  unscientific, and the most useful data available in month one

## How the three divide the work

| | Job 1 · Nudge | Job 2 · Cold | Job 3 · Verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience size | Dozens | Hundreds | Dozens, mid-decision |
| Speed to return | Weeks | 6–12 months | **Immediate** |
| Effort | Low | High | Medium, one-off |
| Primary home | LinkedIn, email | LinkedIn, SEO | Website |
| Share of content | ~20% | ~60% | ~20% + the site |
| Measured by | Second-department sales | Cold enquiries, branded search | Credentials downloads, referral conversion |
| Looks like failure when | Reach is low *(it will be)* | Nothing happens for months *(it won't)* | Nothing is ever attributed *(it can't be)* |

**Build order: Job 3, then Job 1, then Job 2.** Verification is a one-off build
that unblocks revenue this month. Nudging is cheap and warm. Cold reach is the
long game and it can start while the other two are already paying.

---

## The rule

Every piece of content declares its job. A piece serving all three serves none
— the audiences want different things and the register is different for each.
Job 1 assumes we're trusted. Job 2 assumes we're unknown. Job 3 assumes we're
being checked.

When the monthly review shows a piece underperforming, **the first question is
which job it was doing** — not whether it got engagement. A Job 1 post with 200
impressions that sold a lighting package outperformed everything else we made
that month.
