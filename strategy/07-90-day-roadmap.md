# 90-Day Roadmap

Sequenced so that nothing depends on something that doesn't exist yet.

---

## Weeks 1–2 — Foundations (no publishing)

The temptation is to start posting immediately. Don't. Two weeks of setup
makes the following eleven weeks worth doing.

- [ ] **Fill in `00-brand-brief.md`** — blocks everything else
- [ ] Build the questions bank: 20 real client questions (`questions-bank.md`)
- [ ] Voice calibration: 2 samples of existing writing that sound right
- [ ] Complete all three company profiles (`08-profile-buildout.md`)
- [ ] Complete director LinkedIn profiles (`09-director-linkedin.md`)
- [ ] Website: sitemap signed off, build started
- [ ] GA4, Search Console, CRM source field, UTM convention live
- [ ] **Set up passive photo capture** — one shared folder, crew take phone
      photos at every get-in and load-out. Nobody in delivery is going to
      schedule a shoot, and they don't need to: this industry photographs
      itself. One properly organised folder feeds Instagram and Facebook for
      a year
- [ ] Ask the existing client base for reviews and testimonials. Ten years of
      clients have never been asked — it's the easiest win available and, per
      §3, reviews matter competitively in this niche

## Weeks 3–4 — Website live, content begins

- [ ] Website phase-1 pages live, including 3 case studies
- [ ] Analytics verified — test a form submission end to end before trusting it
- [ ] First 4 blog posts drafted (a month's buffer before publishing starts)
- [ ] First director voice-note session recorded → 6–8 posts of raw material
- [ ] Publishing starts: **week 4, Slot A**

## Weeks 5–8 — Rhythm

- [ ] Full cadence running: 1 blog + 3 company social + 3 director posts/week
- [ ] Weekly metrics capture, every Monday, without fail
- [ ] Ask 5 clients for Facebook/Google reviews
- [ ] **End of week 8: first monthly review** (`analytics/monthly-review.md`)
      — scores are provisional at this volume, and that's fine. The habit is
      what's being established, not the conclusion
- [ ] First "next month's bets" written

## Weeks 9–12 — Data starts steering

- [ ] Calendar built from the 60/30/10 allocation rather than from ideas
- [ ] Retire the worst-performing format; double the best
- [ ] Search Console reviewed — first keyword opportunities appearing
- [ ] Second monthly review, with real comparative data
- [ ] Decide on: newsletter, paid amplification of the top 2 posts, phase-2
      website pages. All three decisions made from the data, not from
      preference

## What "working" looks like at day 90

Honest expectations for a UK B2B account starting from zero:

| | Realistic at 90 days |
|---|---|
| Blog posts published | 8–10 |
| Company LinkedIn followers | +100–300, if they're the right ones |
| Director post reach | 1,000–5,000 per post once warmed up |
| Website sessions/month | 200–800 |
| Qualified enquiries from content | 1–5 |
| Organic search traffic | Barely started — this is normal |

The 90-day number that actually matters is none of the above. It's whether
the system runs without heroics: profiles complete, cadence held for eight
consecutive weeks, two monthly reviews done, and decisions being made from
the metrics log rather than from opinion.

Content compounds. Months 4–9 are where it pays, and only for people who
were still publishing in month 3.

---

## A note on what this is actually for

This business reached £1m in ten years with no website, no business cards and
no marketing, growing 50% a year since Covid. The content plan isn't fixing
something broken — it's continuing that curve by making a reputation which
already works legible to people who can't currently see it, and by giving
every past client something to point at when they recommend us.

That reframes the day-90 numbers above. A handful of enquiries is a normal
result. The referrer who finally has a link to send, and the agency that
checked us out before calling, won't show up in any of those rows — and
they're most of the value.
